---
name: TOMI visual and marketplace direction
description: Stakeholder direction for site identity, regional illustration, programme covers and the proposed commercial marketplace.
---

The stakeholder wants an African identity inspired by Wakanda and Kuba textiles from DRC across the ecosystem, with the actual TOMI logo in the header instead of a typed wordmark. Use the supplied green/gold Emirates skyline artwork with motion; Africa should feature African monuments including pyramids, and Europe the Eiffel Tower. Illustrative regional composites must not masquerade as photographs.

**Why:** The stakeholder supplied the backgrounds and explicitly corrected the header and region-specific visual direction.

**How to apply:** Keep patterns readable, motion pausable and reduced-motion aware. Do not imply Marvel affiliation or historical authenticity for original geometric artwork.

The stakeholder supplied the standalone three-line THE ONE MOVEMENT INTERNATIONAL logo to replace the previous peace/slogan banner and the platform's generic icon plus typed name. Members must be able to explore the public site without signing out and return to their personal ecosystem; navigation must not trap them in their account.

**Why:** Their screenshots identified both header treatments; they subsequently clarified that signed-in members must still be able to discover the public site. Subscription-dependent privileges do not require restricting public discovery.

**How to apply:** Use the supplied mark, not a recreated wordmark. Keep it legible on dark headers without an ornamental frame; show real subscription-based access without conflating payment with business approval or certification.

The stakeholder approved the regional backgrounds and animations but explicitly asked to remove the repeated ornamental contours and keep internal editorial/work-in-progress text off public pages.

**Why:** Their mobile screenshots showed repeated triangular header/hero/footer trim, giant letterboxing around posters, cropped faces and visible missing-data/editorial notes.

**How to apply:** Preserve the approved backgrounds and motion when refining presentation. Keep imagery properly framed and public copy visitor-facing; do not reintroduce decorative perimeter bands or expose workflow notes. Retain concise factual statuses, legally necessary notices and accessible source/licence attribution rather than replacing them with false confirmations.

The stakeholder designates the supplied Naomi Campbell artwork as The One Awards cover. For the upcoming Dubai Fashion Week, the founder explicitly replaced the earlier three-model cover with his animated horseman artwork, presenting himself as “Chevalier de la paix et de l’unité.”

**Why:** They explicitly corrected which artwork represents each programme, despite wording printed on the posters, and later selected the horseman for the first Dubai edition.

**How to apply:** Keep programme-cover choice separate from any claim about celebrity attendance, casting or awards already conferred.

The stakeholder identifies Dr Denis Mukwege as a TOMI partner and requests certification. The meaning is TOMI's own declared partner recognition, not a verified member account, UN endorsement or Nobel Committee certification.

**Why:** Partner recognition, Nobel laureate status and account verification are different claims.

**How to apply:** Label issuer and scope of badges explicitly. Use the Nobel Committee as the source of the 2018 award.

The stakeholder proposes a multi-brand marketplace for jewellery, accessories and other products, each contracted brand having its own product space, with 5% of sales supporting TOMI humanitarian action.

**Why:** Their stated goal is to let people buy and sell while helping humanity.

The stakeholder has chosen TOMI's own commission as the source of the humanitarian contribution, rather than an additional deduction funded by the seller or a buyer surcharge.

**Why:** They explicitly selected “TOMI, prélevés sur sa propre commission”.

**How to apply:** Humanitarian giving stays separate from professional subscriptions. Do not represent contributions as transferred until they actually are.

The agreed total TOMI sales commission is 5%, and the humanitarian contribution is 5% of the sale price, funded from that commission. Therefore the entire commission is humanitarian; this is not a donation of 5% of the commission.

**Why:** The stakeholder explicitly entered a total commission of 5 and selected “5 % du prix de vente, financés sur la commission TOMI”.

**How to apply:** On a 100-unit product sale, allocate 95 to the seller and 5 to humanitarian purposes before processing fees, taxes, shipping and refund adjustments. TOMI retains no operating margin from this commission. Refund handling, fulfilment details and authorisations must still be settled before activating real payments; do not silently subtract provider fees from the promised humanitarian 5%.

Payment-provider processing fees are paid by the seller and deducted from the seller's 95% share.

**Why:** The stakeholder explicitly selected “Le vendeur, déduits de sa part de 95 %”.

**How to apply:** Seller net proceeds equal the seller allocation minus actual disclosed processing fees. Do not quote a provider fee rate before verifying it, charge the buyer an invented supplement, or reduce the humanitarian allocation to cover fees.

Marketplace sellers and buyers should manage transactions through their own profiles. The stakeholder wants a stylist's subscription to enable product listings, with sellers receiving proceeds in their own bank accounts and uploading a business licence to qualify.

**Why:** They explicitly describe this as the benefit that makes a monthly subscription attractive to brands and stylists, not a TOMI-managed retail catalogue.

**How to apply:** Design seller product/order management and buyer purchase tracking as real account capabilities. Keep business-licence documents private and review them separately from paid membership and community attestations. Bank onboarding belongs with the payment provider, not public profiles or chat.

Sellers may start selling as soon as their monthly subscription is activated, once their business licence is validated and payment-provider requirements are satisfied. No twelve-month waiting period or annual prepayment requirement applies.

**Why:** The stakeholder explicitly selected “Dès l’activation de son abonnement mensuel” in response to the seller-access timing question.

**How to apply:** Treat active monthly membership, approved business licence and authorised payment onboarding as separate eligibility conditions; do not gate sales on membership seniority.
